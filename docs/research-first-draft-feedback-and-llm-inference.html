The bottom line

Five findings, all verified 3-0

01 · WHAT MATTERS

First-draft feedback is higher-order, full stop

Thesis, argument, structure, evidence, rhetorical fit — not surface copyediting. The settled consensus of composition research and writing-center practice.

02 · THE HUMAN GAP

Humans are weak at the labor-intensive part

Reviewers catch ~30% of seeded major errors; two reviewers agree at r≈0.34; ~18% of stats slip through. The bottleneck is exhaustive, consistent coverage.

03 · THE LLM UNLOCK

Systematic claim→evidence checking

RIGOURATE grades over-claiming [0,1]; VeriFastScore verifies 6.6× faster, 39% cheaper. The week-long check compresses to seconds.

04 · THE MOAT

"Does the source actually say this?"

Existence-checking is solved. Source-support checking "remains far less tractable… the gap is wide and probably widening." No commodity tool has cracked it.

05 · THE SAFE SHAPE

LLM-as-questioner, never ghostwriter

The best-supported non-ghostwriting design: static findings repurposed as conversation starters that prompt reflection — an exact match for gaddr's "retrieve, structure, question, annotate" rule.

01

What expert humans
critique on first drafts

Three traditions — composition pedagogy, academic peer review, and editorial craft — and they all point the same direction: global before local.

Sommers 1980 · HOC-before-LOC · reverse outline

Novices and experts mean different things by "revision"

Students conceive revision as word-swapping; experienced writers revise recursively, at the level of meaning and structure.

Sommers, Revision Strategies of Student & Experienced Adult Writers, CCC 1980 · ERIC EJ240356  Verified 3-0

Higher-order concerns come first

Thesis · Organization · Development/Evidence · Audience — then, and only then, grammar. The operational spine of writing-center practice (Reigstad & McAndrew 1984).

Southwestern triage guide  Verified 3-0

The reverse outline

Reconstruct the outline from the draft to expose its as-written skeleton — "a bullet-point view of your paper's structure." Always useful; a pure structuring task, not ghostwriting.

UW–Madison  Verified 3-0

→ gaddr consequence

Steer the writer toward the moves novices skip — argument, structure, evidence. Surfacing grammar first trains exactly the wrong revision reflex. Reverse-outlining is the cheapest, most legible, zero-ghostwriting feature available.

Editing is a hierarchy

Developmental (idea, audience, structure) → line (sentence meaning) → copy (grammar, spelling). Early drafts belong to the top. Contextual

The New Yorker method
Checkers take the piece apart claim-by-claim and rebuild it against original sources — confirming the writer read and represented each source correctly.

Not just names and dates: "the USGS report that he read, he read correctly." Done manually, over days to weeks.

Columbia Journalism Review Contextual

→ This is exactly the check gaddr aspires to automate — the highest-integrity move, and the one that takes a human a week.

02

Unique LLM feedback
that's slow for humans

Retrieval, structuring, questioning, annotation — never ghostwriting. Where a July-2026 model does in seconds what an editor does in a week.

Decompose into claims → check each against evidence

The capability arc, 2023 → 2026

2023 · FActScore

Atomic-fact scoring

Split output into atomic facts; score the share supported. Correlates with human annotation ρ≈0.82. SAFE / LongFact extend to long-form. Contextual

→
JAN 2026 · RIGOURATE

Graded over-claim [0,1]

Retrieves each claim's supporting text/figures and scores whether its strength is proportionally supported — not true/false. "A learnable and evaluable problem." Verified 3-0

→
EMNLP 2025 · VeriFastScore

Fast enough to be interactive

8B model, single pass: 6.6× faster, 39% cheaper retrieval. But: no rationales, ~29% missed claims. Verified 3-0

→ gaddr consequence

Claim-verification can run off the keystroke path affordably and at length — but the constellation view must show provenance and rationale (the raw model omits them) and cannot be assumed to catch every claim. Argues for human-in-the-loop acceptance, never silent auto-annotation.

Every tradition prioritizes the global layer first; LLMs are strongest at the exhaustive, retrieval-heavy version of that work. The overlap sits squarely inside "no ghostwriting."

Designing the constellation review

Seven implications

  1. Order it like a writing-center session: HOC before LOC. Lead with structure/argument/evidence; demote surface notes.
  2. Make the reverse outline a first-class early artifact. "Here's the argument your draft actually makes" — cheapest high-value, zero-ghostwriting move.
  3. Build claim→evidence as a graded annotation, not a verdict. Attach an over-claim score and the evidence span. "Half-supported" beats a red/green stamp.
  4. Treat "does the source say this" as the moat — ship defensively. Show provenance; require human acceptance; never auto-apply.
  1. Frame findings as questions, not corrections — conversation starters the writer answers in their own words.
  2. Keep AI's hands off prose. The homogenization evidence says the voice cost is real and measured. The constraint is a feature.
  3. Everything stays off the keystroke path. Verification is now cheap enough to run async at length — exactly where "typing latency is P0" wants it.
What this research does NOT establish

Limits, gaps, and what to check next

CAVEATS
  • Tractability ≠ solved. RIGOURATE ~0.5 CCC; VeriFastScore r=0.80 with ~29% omission; citation-support 33–80%. Provenance + human accept mandatory.
  • Domain-transfer risk. Peer-review + verification evidence is biomedical/structured. Transfer to open-web general essays is unproven.
  • Part 1C (journalism) is under-verified — quoted, not gated. Re-source before load-bearing use.
  • Recency. Core LLM sources are Jan–May 2026 preprints; figures move fast.
OPEN QUESTIONS
  • Does claim→evidence checking transfer from structured papers to open-web general essays?
  • What's the measured effect of AI vs instructor feedback on revision quality? (The classroom RCTs didn't surface — a real gap.)
  • How well can systems steelman opposing literature and surface the best missed source? Currently unmeasured — and it's a core gaddr feature.
  • What guardrails reliably keep annotation from sliding into ghostwriting or homogenization?
