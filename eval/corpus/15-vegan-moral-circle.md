had the argument again at dinner, the vegan one, and as usual I lost while being sure I was somehow right, which is the worst way to lose. so let me actually look at it instead of performing it.

the case against me is honestly pretty clean. I already believe the premises. I believe suffering is bad, I believe animals suffer, I've met pigs, they're obviously somebody home in there. I would never hurt a dog. and yet I eat things that lived worse lives than any dog I'd rescue. my nephew asked me why it's different and I heard myself give an answer that I would have flunked a student for, something about tradition and protein. the honest answer is habit and taste, which are exactly the reasons every previous moral circle stayed small as long as it did. people two hundred years from now might read our farming statistics the way we read certain old ledgers. that thought lands on me physically when I let it.

and yet I keep not changing, which is itself data. either I don't really believe my premises, or belief just doesn't move behavior the way I assumed, and I suspect the second, which is almost worse. akrasia at industrial scale.

but here's the thread I actually want to pull, is the individual-purity framing even the right one. my not eating a burger doesn't reach any particular animal, the supply chain doesn't feel me. the effective thing was never my plate, it's price and policy and the lab-grown stuff, make the ethical option the cheap default and the whole question dissolves without anyone winning a dinner argument. though I notice how convenient that framing is for me personally, structural change conveniently requires nothing of me tonight. suspicious. maybe the plate isn't leverage but it might be practice, you don't get the structural fighters without people who've made it personal first. don't know. the nephew question is still sitting there.
