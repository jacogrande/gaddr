made bread again this weekend, the whole ceremony, and halfway through the second knead I did the math, four hours of my time for a loaf that costs six dollars at the good bakery, made by someone better at it than me. so why does the homemade one feel like it counts and the bought one doesn't.

it's not taste, the bakery loaf wins blind. it's something about the hours being in it. like the loaf is a receipt for a way I spent a saturday, attention with a crust on it. the store loaf feeds us the same but it's mute, it has nothing to say about my life.

which makes me wonder how many of my from-scratch convictions are actually about the food and how many are about wanting some part of the day to have my fingerprints on it. the garden is probably the same story, eleven dollars of tomatoes, eighty hours. terrible farming, but maybe farming was never the activity. the activity was having something in my week that can't be delivered.
