four years fully remote now and I still can't decide if it's the best thing that happened to my working life or a slow leak I've stopped noticing. the obvious wins are real, no commute, deep mornings, I see my kids at lunch. anyone who says productivity dropped isn't describing me, I ship more than I ever did in an office.

but. the stuff that's gone is exactly the stuff that's hard to point at. I haven't overheard a useful conversation in four years. every interaction I have is scheduled, which means every interaction has an agenda, which means the aimless ones where someone's half-formed idea collides with mine just don't occur. I used to change my mind because of hallway stuff. when did I last change my mind because of a video call.

and there's a career-shaped version of this worry too. the juniors on my team are learning the job through a straw, they see the meetings but not the between, and I genuinely can't tell if they're developing slower or just differently. maybe apprenticeship needs a room. or maybe that's my nostalgia talking, the same way people once insisted you couldn't learn from books, real knowledge needed a master's workshop.

so the position I'm testing is something like, remote work is individually optimal and collectively corrosive, everyone's day gets better while some slow shared thing degrades. tragedy of the commons except the commons is ambient conversation. if that's true the fix isn't return-to-office theater, three mandated days of sitting on zoom in a building, that captures the costs and none of the collisions. the fix would have to actually recreate the collisions on purpose. I don't know what that looks like. maybe it doesn't look like anything and the trade is just a trade.
