I can't read like I used to and I want to figure out whether that sentence is actually true or just a thing people my age say. what I notice: I still read a lot, arguably more than ever, but it's all fragments, threads, newsletters, halves of articles. the long unbroken sitting-with-a-book thing happens maybe twice a year and it used to be weekly. so the total volume went up while the depth went down, if depth is even the right frame.

the standard story is that the phone broke my attention and I'm inclined to believe it because it flatters me, the machine did it, not me. but there's another story where my reading changed because my life changed, I have less unclaimed time, and fragments are what fits in the gaps. under that story the phone is a symptom not a cause. I honestly can't tell which story is true and it bothers me that both feel plausible depending on my mood.

here's the thing I keep circling though. when I do manage a long book, something different happens to my thinking for days after, like a muscle remembering. slower, more connected, I write better. that effect seems real and repeatable. so even if I can't settle the causal story about phones, I can observe that the deep mode produces something the fragment mode doesn't. maybe the right question isn't what broke my attention but what the deep mode is actually for, what it produces that I'm otherwise not producing. testing that framing. it might just be nostalgia with a productivity costume on. but I don't think so, the after-effect is too consistent.
