Every real idea I've had in the last year arrived while walking, and I'm starting to wonder what that's actually about. Not at the desk, where I officially think, with all the thinking equipment. On the long loop through the park, hands empty, mind supposedly off duty. Yesterday the solution to a work thing I'd fought for a week just presented itself between the pond and the tennis courts, fully formed, like it had been waiting there.

The pop-science explanation would be diffuse mode or default mode network or whatever, and fine, probably neurons. But I'm more interested in the shape of the experience. Desk thinking feels like pushing, walk thinking feels like listening. At the desk I hold the problem and squeeze, on the walk I've clearly handed it to someone else in there and they work on it without me. Which raises a slightly unsettling question about who exactly the desk-me thinks he is, if the actual production happens off stage.

There's also the rhythm thing. Something about the meter of footsteps seems to matter, ideas arrive on the beat somehow. All the famous walkers, the philosophers with their fixed daily routes, maybe that wasn't quirk, maybe walking was the technology and the writing was just transcription.

What I can't figure out is whether the walk is generative or just permissive. Does moving produce the thoughts, or does it merely stop me from interrupting them, is walking a stimulant or a restraint on my meddling. Because those imply opposite things. If it's a stimulant I should walk more. If it's a restraint on interference, then the interesting question becomes what else would get out of the way, and whether the desk itself, the posture of trying, is the actual obstacle I've been sitting at for years.
