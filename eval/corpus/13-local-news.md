the paper here is down to two reporters. two. for a city of 300k. the council meets every tuesday and most tuesdays nobody is in the room writing anything down, and I keep trying to articulate why that scares me more than the big national media stuff everyone argues about.

the cliché is democracy dies in darkness which is too grand for what I mean. what I mean is smaller and more mechanical. when nobody's in the room, the cost of a bad decision drops to zero. not corruption necessarily, just laziness, the contract that doesn't get a second bid, the variance that gets waved through. surveillance was doing invisible work, the stories that never needed writing because everyone knew someone might write them. you can't measure prevented stories, which is exactly why nobody would pay for them.

and that's the actual problem isn't it, the business model was always a bundle trick. classifieds and car ads paid for the council reporter, readers never valued the council reporter at her cost, craigslist didn't kill journalism it just unbundled the subsidy and revealed that the civic part never had a market.

so the thing I'm chewing on is what replaces a subsidy that nobody knew was a subsidy. philanthropy does it in some cities, works but feels fragile, whoever funds the watchdog picks the watchdog. government funding has the obvious ouroboros problem, the council funding the council reporter. there are these citizen-run newsletters popping up, one guy with a substack covering zoning, which I love and also worry about, no editor, no correction culture, burns out in eighteen months. maybe the honest framing is that room-sitting is infrastructure, like sewers, boring, essential, unmarketable, and infrastructure has never been funded by asking who'll pay retail for it. I'm not fully convinced by my own analogy yet, sewers don't need independence from the city that funds them. that's the snag.
