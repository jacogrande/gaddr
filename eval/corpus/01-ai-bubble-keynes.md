okay so everyone I read keeps saying the AI buildout is a bubble and honestly the numbers do look insane, hundreds of billions on data centers and the revenue behind it is like a rounding error. but then I keep thinking about railways. the railway manias bankrupted basically everyone who invested and left behind track that powered a century of growth. the dotcom crash left all that fiber that ended up carrying youtube. so maybe bubbles are just how infrastructure gets built faster than any sober plan would allow? investors eat the loss, society keeps the capacity.

except. GPUs are not track. that's the thing I can't get past. track sits there for a hundred years, a GPU is basically obsolete in what, four years, five? so if the crash comes before the deployment phase arrives we're not left with dark fiber waiting to be lit, we're left with warehouses of obsolete silicon and enormous depreciation writeoffs. does the residue argument even work when the asset rots that fast.

and then there's the Keynes angle which I originally wanted to write about and keep drifting away from. if all this capex is what's actually holding up GDP right now, and it stops, what does the government even do. you can't exactly stimulate your way into people wanting more chatbots. or can you, maybe demand is demand. I keep going back and forth on whether this boom is animal spirits in the good sense, the thing that gets big stuff built, or animal spirits in the bad sense, the thing that ends in a crater. I genuinely don't know which side I'd defend if someone pushed me. probably the productive bubble side but I'd be nervous about it.
