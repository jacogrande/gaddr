another maintainer archived his project this week, the note said the usual things, thankless, endless, done. library with forty million downloads, one guy, nights and weekends, a decade. and the same cycle in the comments, someone says pay maintainers, someone says he chose this, someone links the xkcd with the tower of blocks. nothing changes.

the framing I keep testing is that open source pulled off the strangest trick in economics, it got the world's most valuable industry to run its foundations on gift labor, and the gift economy and the money economy are now load-bearing on each other with no agreed exchange rate. the corporation extracts like it's infrastructure, the maintainer gives like it's art, and burnout is what the mismatch feels like from inside.

but I want to be careful because pay-the-maintainers, the obvious fix, has real failure modes. money changes the relationship, once you're paid the entitled issue-filer becomes a customer and maybe that's worse. some maintainers explicitly don't want it, the gift is the point, payment would convert a practice they love into a job they'd hate. sponsorship exists and flows overwhelmingly to the already-visible, the forty-million-download guy got coffee money while framework influencers do fine.

so maybe the unit is wrong. not pay the person, staff the dependency. the big companies know their critical paths, every one of them has an internal list of terrifying single-maintainer dependencies, what if the norm became seconding an engineer, you use it at scale, you contribute headcount not cash. keeps the gift structure, adds redundancy, the maintainer gets colleagues instead of customers. the objection I can already hear is governance capture, whoever staffs it steers it. maybe. but a captured living project might beat a pure dead one, that's the trade I'm turning over.
