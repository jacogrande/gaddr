my sister's kid did four saturdays of test prep and jumped 180 points and the family group chat treated it as a triumph and all I could think was, 180 points of what. what property of a mind changes in four saturdays. not intelligence, obviously. not knowledge, the prep wasn't content, it was tactics, when to guess, how the trap answers are shaped. so the test measures, at least partly, access to the tactics, which is to say money, laundered through a number that presents itself as talent.

that's the easy dunk though and I don't fully trust it, because the counterargument has teeth. the SAT is the villain everyone can see, but the alternative isn't some pure measure, it's essays polished by consultants, extracurriculars that require a parent with a car and free afternoons, interviews that reward kids raised in the dialect of institutions. tests are gameable for thousands of dollars, the holistic stuff is gameable for hundreds of thousands. there's apparently decent evidence the test, for all its tilt, finds poor bright kids that the soft criteria miss. the school that went test-optional didn't get more equal, it got more opaque, and opacity always pays the insiders.

so the live question for me isn't test versus no test, it's which kind of unfairness you can see and therefore correct. a biased ruler that everyone can inspect might beat a vibes committee whose bias hides in adjectives. you can norm a score against circumstance, publish the tilt, argue about it in daylight. you can't audit an impression. I notice this is the same shape as an argument I make about algorithms versus human judges, legible bias versus illegible bias, and I keep landing on legible. either I've found a principle or I've found a rut. worth checking which.
