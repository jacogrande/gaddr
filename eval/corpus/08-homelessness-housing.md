the encampment by the underpass got cleared again this week, third time this year, and everyone I know had one of the same two reactions. one group says finally, it was unsafe, and the other says cruelty, they just moved suffering out of view. and I notice I have both reactions at once which probably means I haven't actually thought it through.

so let me try. the thing I keep hearing is that homelessness is fundamentally a housing problem, houston apparently just gave people apartments and the numbers dropped a lot. the housing-first logic is that you can't stabilize anything else, addiction, employment, health, while someone is sleeping outside, the housing is the floor everything else stands on. that makes sense to me mechanically.

but when I say it out loud the objection I hear back is always the same, the guy screaming at traffic isn't going to be fixed by a lease. and honestly the objection isn't nothing, some visible street homelessness is severe mental illness and a unit with a door doesn't treat psychosis. the question is proportions. if that guy is ten percent of the problem but a hundred percent of what people picture, then policy made for the picture will miss the problem. most of it is apparently invisible, cars, couches, motels, people one paycheck out. rent went up, wages didn't, some people fell through, boring arithmetic, no demons required.

what I'm testing is whether both camps are right about different populations and the entire public fight comes from using one word for two situations. clearing camps treats the visible ten percent punitively and does nothing for the arithmetic ninety. housing first treats the arithmetic ninety well and maybe undertreats the acute cases. if that's the real shape then the argument I keep having at dinner is fake, we're each holding a different slice and calling it the whole. I'd want actual numbers on the proportions before I trust this, but it's the first framing that's made the shouting make sense to me.
