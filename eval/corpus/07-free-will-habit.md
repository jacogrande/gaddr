Something happened this morning that I can't stop turning over. I was out the door and halfway down the block before I noticed I was walking to the bakery, no decision anywhere in sight, the legs just went. And the thought that followed me the rest of the walk was, how much of my day is like that. Not the dramatic version of the free will question, whether physics permits choice, I find that version weirdly boring, but the practical version. What fraction of what I do today will involve anything that deserves to be called deciding.

Honest answer, probably under five percent. The coffee, the route, the opening of the laptop, the checking of the thing, the tone I take with the first email. All grooves. And here's where it gets interesting to me, I don't experience the grooves as unfreedom. They feel like me. The habits are not obstacles between me and my will, they sort of are my will, sedimented. Aristotle apparently thought something like this, that character just is habit accumulated, though I only half remember it and might be flattening him.

But if character is sediment then the question moves upstream. Who laid the sediment down? Some of it me, deliberately, I built the morning writing habit brick by brick and I'm proud of it. But most of it was laid by whoever designed my defaults, the apps that trained the checking reflex, the office culture that trained the tone, my parents, the city. So the free will question stops being about the moment of choice and becomes about something like authorship of the groove. Freedom isn't choosing in the moment, it's having been the one who dug your own channels.

Wait, but that has a regress problem, obviously. Whoever dug the channels was themselves already channeled. The me who built the writing habit was already the kind of person who builds writing habits, and I didn't choose to be that kind of person. So either freedom bottoms out somewhere, some unmoved chooser, which I don't believe in, or it's grooves all the way down and the feeling of authorship is just what a groove feels like from inside.

And yet. There's this thing that happens, rarely, where a groove becomes visible. Like this morning, catching my legs walking to the bakery. In the moment of catching it, something is briefly possible that wasn't possible a second before, I can go along or not go along. The noticing seems to matter, it's the only place anything like freedom actually shows up in my experience. Not in the choosing, in the noticing. Which would mean freedom is not a metaphysical property, it's more like a skill, or an event, and it's scarce, and most of what I call my decisions are just grooves narrating themselves.

I don't know what follows from that. Maybe the practical upshot is that arguing about determinism is beside the point and the real question is what makes the noticing happen more often. Interruption? Writing like this? Someone asking the right question at the right moment? That last one is interesting. Other people might be the main technology for making grooves visible. Living alone with your habits is like being a fish deciding whether water exists.
